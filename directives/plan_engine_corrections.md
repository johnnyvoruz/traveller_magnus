## 1. Objective and authority

Johnny's governing principle is that stored physical data must agree with
the published UWP and with itself. Correct definite bugs and apply the
local rules without asking him to decide implementation details. Only the
six policy gaps in section 4 require answers. Recommendations there are
proposals, not adopted Traveller rules.

Read: handoff sections 58 and 60; Agent B's
`findings/regina_liquid_temperature.md`; `findings/tempband_trace.md`;
the local placement, atmosphere, hydrographics, thermal, rotation and
satellite rules; then the engine ranges those findings cite. The findings
are the investigation baseline, not a reason to retrace every engine.
Line references below refer to this checkout on 2026-10-04.

The two tiers concern stored data, not the vanilla/enhanced rendering
switch. Tier 1 reconciles existing stored results. Tier 2 corrects their
generation. Rendering reads the corrected data after publication. Neither
tier uses a latitude-temperature formula from a rendering proposal, an
outside reviewer's Kelvin thresholds, or rules recalled from memory.

Scope: MgT2E trees generated by flesh/top-down and the shared relevant
bottom-up paths. Other editions retain their own rules and golden tests.
No inference of MgT2E climate for an ungenerated chart row.

**Never re-roll hydrographics.** Published mainworld Hydro is input, not a
new random variable. Reconciliation preserves all existing hydro values.
Tier 2 inherits chart Hydro before physical generation; it does not
generate a replacement UWP, repeatedly roll for a match, or alter Hydro
to solve a placement/temperature problem. Other worlds' initial Hydro
generation remains an initial generation step, never a thermal retry.

## 3. Tier 1: reconciliation of stored trees

### 3.1 Pure transformation

Proposed module: `packages/engines/src/reconcile_environment.js`, with a
pure `reconcileTree(tree, policy)` boundary. `policy` is immutable,
versioned rule data and accepted answers, not ambient settings. Result:
`{ tree, changes, diagnostics }`. No RNG, clock, network, engine run,
name allocation or mutation of the input. No imports from generation
into engines. The generation package imports this module for both
derived builds and the final generation hook.

Walk every eligible body and moon exactly once; preserve array order,
body identities and chart summaries. Reuse an existing object when it
does not change. Serialize with the existing canonical serializer.
Require idempotence: reconciling the result again changes no bytes and
emits no additional changes. Preserve original values once in a small
versioned reconciliation provenance object; a rerun must not overwrite
the original with the already-corrected value. Detailed reports live
outside the body objects, keyed by hex and existing body path.

The write allowlist is orbitalTempBand and its provenance,
surfaceTempBand, reconciled liquidType and explicit liquid/phase status,
plus reconciliation metadata. Existing tempBand remains a legacy alias
for compatibility, not the new Climate field. Tier 1 never changes UWP,
atm/atmCode, hydro/hydroCode/hydroPercent, orbital placement, stellar
properties, albedo, greenhouse, temperature numbers, seismicStress,
life, social rolls, manual values or generated trade codes.

### 3.2 Preserve the early band without inventing its history

Future generation stores `orbitalTempBand` immediately on the return
from getTempBand, before the runaway override. Keep the early band
actually used for Hydro DMs separately as `hydroTempBand`: it is the
orbital band unless the existing runaway branch sets Boiling. Retain
runawayGreenhouse independently. This distinction prevents the new name
from falsifying the existing Hydro calculation.

For an old stored tree, retain original tempBand in provenance. Where
the original orbitId and effective HZCO are present, apply the same pure
orbit-deviation classifier with DATA:24-29 to reconstruct orbitalTempBand.
Mark its provenance as reconstructed, not captured. Do not assume that
a stored Boiling tag was the original orbital band; it may be runaway.
If inputs are missing or their historical identity cannot be established,
leave orbitalTempBand unknown with a diagnostic rather than labelling
the runaway result as the original orbit. No randomness or orbital
regeneration is needed or allowed for this reconstruction.

`surfaceTempBand` uses only a valid final meanTempK and the accepted Q1
table. Missing/non-finite temperatures produce an explicit unknown
status. No default 288 K, inherited Frozen label or renderer threshold.
Define every interval and boundary in the policy; test immediately below,
at and above each boundary. The engine and derived path call the same
function, and the UI never implements its own Kelvin classifier.

### 3.3 Liquid identity and phase

First validate hydroPercent as a finite number in [0,100]. Explicit zero
sets active `liquidType` to null and status `none`, including an old Ice
or Unknown Exotic Liquid. Positive coverage is not erased to repair a
thermal conflict. A missing/invalid percentage is unknown, not zero:
emit a blocking diagnostic for a body whose liquid needs validation.
Do not synthesize a precise percentage from the UWP in Tier 1. In
particular, Hydro digit 0 can coexist with a positive rolled percentage.

For positive coverage and valid temperatures, look up the original
substance in DATA.exoticLiquids. Keep its identity when it meets the
accepted Q3 test; then derive range/phase annotations from final low,
mean and high. Validate low <= mean <= high when all exist; inverted
ranges remain diagnostics, never silently sorted into plausible data.
Do not apply a new pressure correction to the table's limits.

Proposed outcomes, subject only to the identified ASK policies:

| Stored value | Reconciled result |
|---|---|
| Known liquid still valid | Same liquidType; record phase variation if relevant |
| Known liquid outside the accepted window | Deterministic Q2 replacement among eligible substances, or explicit unresolved status |
| Ice, zero coverage | liquidType null; status none |
| Ice, positive coverage, valid fully frozen water under Q3 | Keep Ice as the backwards-compatible label; normalized substance Water, phase solid |
| Ice, positive coverage, temperatures allow water rather than permanent ice | Water with phase annotations if Q3 permits; otherwise Q2 repair or unresolved |
| Unknown Exotic Liquid, positive coverage | Re-evaluate final candidates; Q2 selects deterministically if possible; otherwise liquidType null, status unresolved, original string retained in provenance |
| Missing fields or incompatible manually fixed constraints | Preserve original in provenance, explicit unresolved status, no fictitious substance |

An unresolved status must be visible to consumers and counted in the
release report; null/unresolved does not mean a dry world. No magic
unknown substance with fabricated freezing/boiling points. Magma is not
an automatic fallback: HYD:20 says it *may* occur above 1000 K, not that
every empty candidate set should become magma or that it has table
limits. Keep such cases unresolved pending an applicable rule.

### 3.4 Limits and publication contract

Tier 1 is a repair to labels/liquids, not certification of the whole
physical system. It will still report the atmosphere/Hydro mismatches
that only Tier 2 may fix. Keep an exact residual-conflict ledger, not a
generic “all valid” flag. An unresolved liquid or unavailable climate
classification cannot be hidden by a passing test that skips nulls.
Require explicit reviewed counts and a partial-reconciliation status if
publishing a Tier 1 version before Tier 2; a fully consistent release
requires zero unresolved material conflicts.

The UI displays surfaceTempBand as Climate and orbitalTempBand as
Orbital zone, with numeric temperature available when the band is
unknown. Enhanced surfaces consume the corrected substance and coverage.
Vanilla pixel parity still means identical explicit inputs produce
identical output, not that changing truth inputs preserves old images.
The last engine step invokes reconciliation after all temperature,
social-merge and serialization preparation writes, so a later merge
cannot undo it. It must not call the generation engine from a browser.

## 4. The six ASK decisions

These are the only product/rules decisions requested. None asks Johnny
to choose a module boundary, hash format, scheduling strategy or test.
No dependent implementation silently adopts the recommendation.

### Q1 / A01: names for actual surface temperature

**Question:** Should Climate use a new Kelvin-to-label table you supply
or approve, or show just the final temperature until such a table exists?

Options: (a) approve a dedicated MgT2E surface-band table; (b) numerical
temperature only, with the surface band explicitly unclassified.
**Pick:** (a) for the finished feature; use (b) until approved. The plan
does not invent candidate numerical breakpoints. DATA:24-29 is an orbit
table, and renderer tempBandFromKelvin is not a rules source. This is the
specific dependency preventing a fully populated surfaceTempBand release.

### Q2 / A02: replace an impossible liquid without re-rolling

**Question:** When the old liquid is impossible at the final temperature,
should we choose the most abundant compatible listed substance, or leave
the substance unidentified for review?

Options: (a) choose the highest-abundance eligible row, ties in fixed
table order; (b) leave every invalid choice unresolved.
**Pick:** (a), retaining any valid original choice first. No eligible
row means unresolved under either option. Unknown Exotic Liquid goes
through the same policy. Abundance weights exist in DATA:739-755, but
using their maximum for repair is a proposed rule, not the weighted
random algorithm. Chemical/atmospheric eligibility must also pass Q3;
do not turn breathable chart worlds into acid worlds solely to fit heat.

### Q3 / A03: what the liquid label promises

**Question:** Does a world's liquid name describe what can be liquid at
its mean temperature, allowing seasonal freezing/boiling, or must it
remain liquid across the entire low-to-high range?

Options: (a) mean-temperature identity with explicit phase changes at
the extremes; (b) require the entire range inside the liquid window.
**Pick:** (a). Freeze/boil annotations use both endpoints; fully frozen
water uses Ice only when the final high is below the water melting point.
The no-liquid-in-vacuum safeguard stays; atmosphere 0/1 must not force
Ice on a hot world regardless of its temperatures. If that leaves no
allowed substance, report a physical inconsistency for Tier 2.

This answer must include approval of the MgT2E phase/eligibility policy:
keep the existing atmosphere 10-12 exotic candidate restriction initially,
water/ice for the ordinary path, and mark impossible ordinary worlds
unresolved rather than silently converting them to exotics. Local CT and
T5 vacuum-ice statements are not a licence to invent a MgT2E rule. Table
melting/boiling values are RULE; final mean versus full-range identity,
phase-boundary inclusivity and precedence of the MgT2E ice shortcut are
ASK. At the table limits, recommend liquid allowed inclusively, matching
the existing candidate filter.

### Q4 / A04: chart gives no climate

**Question:** For a charted world with no climate supplied, should we
seek a legal orbit compatible with its published atmosphere and Hydro,
or continue assuming the old middle temperature roll?

Options: (a) compatibility-led placement with no claim that the chart
specified Temperate; (b) explicitly adopt the old modified roll 7 default.
**Pick:** (a). Use the accepted Q3 liquid/phase constraints and the chart
atmosphere's rules; do not assume every atmosphere 2-9 world must be
Earthlike. Dry worlds do not acquire a water-temperature requirement.
An explicit valid chart climate adds a constraint; an absent one remains
absent. ORB:20-21 governs reverse engineering when a target is known,
but it does not decide the missing-target case.

### Q5 / A05: the desired orbit is forbidden or incompatible

**Question:** If the generated system has no legal placement matching
the chart's physical requirements, should it stop for review, or may it
change albedo/greenhouse beyond their generated values to force a fit?

Options: (a) search legal placements deterministically with the existing
rolls, then mark the system unresolved if none works; (b) authorize a
new climate-adjustment rule with specified allowed bounds.
**Pick:** (a). Order by distance from the intended baseline, then orbit
number and stable slot identity. Preserve Sa by moving its parent/anchor
as a unit. No new stars, erased exclusion zones, made-up albedo, repeated
dice or rewritten chart Hydro. A complete release waits for resolution
of unsatisfiable systems; it does not silently drop them. The CT climate
deviation text does not establish option (b) for MgT2E.

### Q6 / A06: existing heat safety caps

**Question:** Should we retain the existing component limits (B at 1000,
C at 150), or supply a replacement heat-limit rule?

Options: (a) explicitly adopt the current component caps, with no cap on
their sum; (b) supply a new component/total-heat rule and its values.
**Pick:** (a) for this pass. It minimizes unrelated changes. The final
temperature must still drive liquid validation, however high it becomes.
Do not interpret C's 150 limit as a total stress/temperature ceiling.

## 5. Tier 2: correct generation and its dependencies

### 5.1 Authoritative chart constraints

Proposed `mainworld_constraints.js` takes the published UWP as the
authority, decoded once. Existing chart aliases are checked against it
before generation. Identify the mainworld explicitly through placement,
moon copies and social merges; do not depend on a mutable type string.
Pass `{ mainworldBase, mainworldIdentity, mode }` explicitly to sweeps.

Set physical size/atmosphere/Hydro from these constraints before pressure,
composition, albedo, greenhouse, temperature or life uses them. Never
restore only the display aliases at the end. All duplicated physical
digits and UWP encodings must agree; derived trade codes use those same
digits. Published remarks remain separately identified as chart input,
not overwritten wholesale by computed trade codes. Replace the broad
Object.assign ownership with explicit field selection.

A code lock alone is insufficient if pressure/composition says otherwise.
ATM:17-21 specifies pressure and oxygen constraints, including an
auto-taint result for newly rolled worlds. On an immutable chart mainworld,
generate subordinate properties consistent with the fixed atmosphere;
do not invoke that result to rewrite the published code. If the supplied
rules cannot produce compatible subordinate properties under the accepted
placement, flag the conflict under Q5. No silent clamping of pressure,
oxygen or composition just to pass the digit test.

Hydro code comes from the chart, not a fresh Hydro roll. The normal
percentage draw is used once within that fixed code's existing mapping;
its position and reuse are covered by the RNG plan. On stored correction
fixtures, retain the existing percentage only if compatible with the
authoritative code; changing a rolled percentage belongs to Tier 2 and
must be recorded. Do not patch a stored mismatch in Tier 1.

### 5.2 One thermal evaluator, one final writer

Proposed pure `thermal_context.js` resolves the body's illuminating star
or explicitly represented P-type source set, and its stellar AU. “One
star” means one consistent context, not erasing binary illumination.
Use the existing preliminary P-type source selection consistently as
the initial bug fix; this pass does not invent a different multi-star
irradiance model. A moon inherits its parent's stellar context/AU and
keeps its own satellite distance separately. Missing/invalid context is
a diagnostic, not a default Sun at an arbitrary distance.

Proposed pure `thermal_state.js` consumes that context, final albedo,
greenhouse, rotation, eccentricity, hydro/distribution, pressure and
inherent heat. It computes mean/high/low together, using one shared set
of helpers and caps. Do not add inherent heat again to a previously
heated mean: calculate the solar term from inputs, then combine the
fourth powers once. Respect manual final temperature locks and reject
inconsistent triples; do not let a later biosphere pass overwrite them.
Derived display Celsius follows the project's adopted conversion and
must not become another independently maintained thermal state.

Use ROT:21-24 for the day factor in every evaluation. Distinguish lock
target, use the absolute solar-day magnitude for the square root, and
test long/retrograde days and parent-locked moons. Apply Q6's single
component policy once. Preserve the other existing modifiers pending
specific evidence of a rule conflict; “one formula” is not permission
to replace the MgT2E calculation with a new climate simulation.

Generation still needs a provisional thermal estimate for atmosphere
retention and runaway evaluation. Keep it in a named provisional result,
not the published final field. Both provisional and final stages use
the same context and evaluator with their explicit available inputs.
Separate geology/inherent-heat preparation from temperature-dependent
biosphere evaluation: final temperature and liquid precede biomass,
habitability and other dependants. No downstream field may knowingly
refer to an obsolete provisional temperature. Stage-dependency tests
cover this, rather than fixing labels after leaving life contradictory.

### 5.3 Liquid after temperature; reconciliation last

For new generation, select the initial substance only after final
temperature and Hydro coverage are known. Eligible exotics still use
the existing abundance weights and their assigned random variate; the
timing changes, not the table. Reuse a reserved old draw where available,
never re-roll until a substance happens to fit. Zero coverage produces
none. Q3 governs ordinary water/ice and phase status. No candidate is a
generation conflict, not an invented chemical.

Final reconciliation then validates rather than normally repairing new
generation. It executes after social merges too. This same pure function
is the Tier 1 transformer. New valid selections should be unchanged by it;
a material repair during fresh generation is evidence that the pipeline
still disagrees and must be reported.

### 5.4 Placement and compatibility

Preserve a supplied orbital climate target and the existing rules-based
reverse engineering. Without one, invoke the accepted Q4 policy. Apply
all MgT2E exclusions and actual slot occupancy before accepting a
baseline; recheck the final slot, not just the intended baseline float.
Boundary roll 7 cannot leave it forbidden. Preserve lunar preallocation
and inventory accounting required by ORB:22-26.

Under the recommended Q5 policy, evaluate candidates without RNG using
already assigned roll values and the unified thermal evaluator. Keep
an existing valid placement. For an invalid one, search legal candidate
slots in a fixed order and evaluate the complete physical constraints,
including the accepted Q3 phase windows. Re-evaluate dependent distances,
periods and thermal properties as a coherent candidate state, never patch
only meanTempK. Commit a single compatible candidate or return a named
conflict. Candidate evaluation has a finite slot list and no retry draws.

Do not impose invented “habitable” temperatures on all chart worlds.
Which atmosphere/liquid constraints apply comes from the local rules
and Q3/Q4, not the desired appearance of Regina. It is possible for a
chart and a rolled system to be jointly unsatisfiable; Q5 is the honest
resolution path. There is no promise that every bad tree can be made
consistent without changing a generated quantity.

## 6. RNG compatibility and estimated impact

### 6.1 What can stay identical

Tier 1 makes **zero new random draws** and changes only allowlisted
fields. It must leave all old rolled fields byte-identical.

Tier 2 cannot promise global v5 draw-order preservation. Engines share
an RNG binding within a system. Fixing locks changes branch execution
and removes the erroneous mainworld Hydro roll. Different atmosphere
branches use different draws, and moving the liquid choice changes a
conditional draw: the old candidate list may be empty while the new one
is not, or vice versa. Placement can change the body sequence as well.
Blindly inserting a fixed number of burn draws cannot preserve every
branch. A fresh seeded random value hidden in reconciliation would
violate Tier 1 even if its output were deterministic.

First instrument draw ordinal, stage and body identity in a test-only
trace. Preserve call order where practical: numerical thermal repairs
consume no RNG; a previously drawn liquid variate can be captured and
applied later. Record rather than re-roll any existing Hydro percentage
variate. For mainworld code locking, never call a new Hydro roll to obtain
the desired digit; any compatibility advancement is stream accounting,
not a generated Hydro value, and must be explicit in the trace.

Recommended migration experiment: use the unchanged legacy generator
as a test oracle to produce a semantic draw ledger for the fixture cases.
Match corrected stages against it. Preserve old variates for unchanged
semantic events; new events use an explicitly versioned independent
correction stream, never steal later events' draws. Do not promise or
ship full replay machinery before measuring its cost and correctness.
If replay requires running an entire second legacy engine in production,
prefer an honest engine-version break to that permanent complexity.
Stage/body streams introduced without replay would also change old
values; they are not a claim of compatibility.

Unchanged systems can remain byte-identical in their physical body
payloads when their inputs, branches and all draws are unchanged.
Unchanged worlds inside a changed system are a harder case and are not
guaranteed by the per-hex seed. Generation/index.ts:57-58 reseeds each
hex, so changed draws in Regina need not perturb the next system.
Names and any shared registries must also pass forward/reverse sector
order and slice-size tests before claiming that independence.

### 6.2 Estimate, with its limits

The universe has **180,312 chart rows, 156,222 generated trees and
24,090 partial rows** (handoff sections 15/20/39 and the recorded release
counts). Do not count partial rows as generated systems to be re-rolled.

Plan storage and validation capacity for **all 156,222 trees** changing
hash: Tier 1 adds versioned band/provenance fields to eligible bodies,
and Tier 2 changes engine metadata as well. The 24,090 partial rows keep
their published data. This is an upper-bound planning estimate, not a
measured count of changed climates.

For substantive Tier 2 physical changes, budget **roughly 125,000 to
156,222 systems (80-100% of generated trees)**. This is a deliberately
broad engineering estimate, not a statistical confidence interval: the
broken flesh-base contract affects every chart-derived mainworld path,
and temperature/liquid fixes affect other bodies too. Some random results
already agree and may survive unchanged. B's 30/439 zero-coverage Ice
mainworlds and the other Marches totals establish widespread defects but
are not a representative universe-wide sample and do not determine that
percentage. Do not extrapolate an exact count from Regina or Marches.

Replace this estimate before release with: a no-generation Tier 1 scan
of all available stored trees; a paired old/new run of whole Marches;
then a stratified sample across sectors, stellar multiplicity, lunar
mainworlds, atmosphere/Hydro classes and partial status. Finally report
exact counts from the full shadow build. Distinguish hashes changed only
by metadata, label/liquid-only changes, mainworld physical changes,
other-body changes and RNG-cascade changes. No implementation or such
measurement was performed while writing this plan.
